# Income from House Property — FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27
# Source: Income Tax Act 1961, Sections 22-27, Finance Act 2025

Income from House Property is a separate head of income under Section 22 of the
Income Tax Act. It covers income from renting out a property or the annual value
of any property owned by the taxpayer.

---

## How Rental Income is Computed (Step by Step)

The computation is the same under both Old and New Tax Regimes for let-out property.

### Step 1: Gross Annual Value (GAV)
The higher of:
- Actual rent received or receivable
- Expected/fair market rent of the property

If property is vacant for part of the year, actual rent for the occupied period is used.

### Step 2: Net Annual Value (NAV)
NAV = Gross Annual Value - Municipal Taxes paid by the owner during the year

**Important:** Only municipal taxes actually paid by the owner (not the tenant) can be deducted.

### Step 3: Section 24(a) — Standard Deduction
Standard Deduction = 30% of NAV

**This is a flat deduction. No bills or proof required.**
It is meant to cover maintenance, repairs, and other property-related costs.
It is available for ALL let-out properties automatically.

### Step 4: Section 24(b) — Home Loan Interest Deduction

#### For Let-Out Property (Property given on rent):
- Deduction = Actual interest paid on home loan (no upper limit)
- There is NO cap of Rs.2,00,000 for let-out properties
- The full interest amount is deductible while computing HP income

#### For Self-Occupied Property:
- Maximum deduction capped at Rs.2,00,000 per year
- Property must be self-occupied (not rented out)

### Step 5: Taxable Income from House Property

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross Annual Rent | Rs.X |
| Less: Municipal Taxes paid | (Rs.Y) |
| = Net Annual Value (NAV) | Rs.(X - Y) |
| Less: 30% of NAV (Section 24(a)) | (Rs.0.30 * NAV) |
| Less: Interest on Home Loan (Section 24(b)) | (Rs.Z) |
| = Income from House Property | Rs.Result |

Result can be positive (taxable) or negative (loss from HP).

---

## Worked Example: Gross Rent Rs.15,00,000

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Gross Annual Rent | Actual rent | Rs.15,00,000 |
| Less: Municipal Taxes | Paid by owner | Rs.20,000 |
| = Net Annual Value (NAV) | | Rs.14,80,000 |
| Less: Section 24(a) — 30% of NAV | 30% of 14,80,000 | Rs.4,44,000 |
| Less: Section 24(b) — Home Loan Interest | Actual paid (e.g., Rs.3,00,000) | Rs.3,00,000 |
| = Taxable Income from House Property | | Rs.7,36,000 |

**Final taxable HP income = Rs.7,36,000** (not Rs.15,00,000)

## Loss from House Property and Set-Off Rules

If interest paid exceeds rent minus deductions, HP income becomes negative (a loss).

### Example of HP Loss:
| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross Rent | Rs.15,00,000 |
| Municipal Taxes | Rs.20,000 |
| NAV | Rs.14,80,000 |
| Section 24(a) — 30% of NAV | Rs.4,44,000 |
| Section 24(b) — Home Loan Interest | Rs.12,00,000 |
| Income from HP | -Rs.1,64,000 (LOSS) |

### Set-Off Rules (Section 71B):
1. **Current Year Set-Off**: Loss from House Property can be set off against salary,
   business income, or other heads of income in the same year.
   **Maximum: Rs.2,00,000 per year.**
2. **Carry Forward**: Any remaining loss (beyond Rs.2L) cannot be set off against
   other income heads. It is carried forward for up to **8 assessment years** and can
   only be set off against future Income from House Property.

**IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:**
- The interest DEDUCTION under Section 24(b) for let-out property is NOT capped.
- But the LOSS SET-OFF against other income heads IS capped at Rs.2 lakh per year.
- These are two different rules that must not be confused.

## New Tax Regime vs Old Tax Regime — Rental Income

| Deduction | Old Regime | New Regime |
|---|---|---|
| Municipal Taxes | Allowed | Allowed |
| Section 24(a) — 30% standard deduction | Allowed | Allowed |
| Section 24(b) — Home loan interest (let-out) | Allowed (no cap) | Allowed (no cap) |
| Section 24(b) — Home loan interest (self-occupied) | Allowed (up to Rs.2L) | NOT allowed |
| Section 80C (ELSS, PPF, home loan principal) | Allowed | NOT allowed |
| Section 80D (Health Insurance) | Allowed | NOT allowed |
| Section 80EEA (First home buyer extra interest) | Allowed (if eligible) | NOT allowed |

**KEY RULE:** Sections 80C, 80D, 80EEA are Chapter VI-A deductions. They are completely
blocked under the New Tax Regime. Section 24(a) and 24(b) for let-out property are
income computation rules (not Chapter VI-A), so they apply under BOTH regimes.

---

## Self-Occupied Property (No Rental Income)

For a property used by the owner for own residence:
- Annual Value is deemed to be NIL (zero)
- Section 24(a) standard deduction does NOT apply (NAV is zero)
- Section 24(b) interest deduction allowed up to Rs.2,00,000 (Old Regime only)
- Under New Regime: Section 24(b) self-occupied interest NOT allowed

---

## Section 80EEA — First Home Buyer (Old Regime Only)

Eligibility conditions (ALL must be satisfied):
- Home loan sanctioned between 01-Apr-2019 and 31-Mar-2022
- Stamp duty value of property does not exceed Rs.45 lakh
- First time home buyer (no other residential property on date of sanction)
- Not already claiming Section 80EE deduction

Maximum deduction: Rs.1,50,000 per year (over and above Section 24(b))
**NOT available under New Tax Regime.**

## Common Misconceptions (Frequently Hallucinated)

1. **WRONG:** "Claim Section 80C under New Regime for rental income."
   **CORRECT:** 80C is blocked under New Regime regardless of income type.

2. **WRONG:** "Section 24(b) is capped at Rs.2 lakh for let-out property."
   **CORRECT:** The Rs.2 lakh cap applies ONLY to self-occupied property. For let-out, actual interest is deductible in full.

3. **WRONG:** "Section 24(a) is optional — you need bills to claim it."
   **CORRECT:** Section 24(a) is a mandatory 30% deduction from NAV. No bills required. It always applies.

4. **WRONG:** "You can deduct the full HP loss against salary income."
   **CORRECT:** HP loss set-off against other income is capped at Rs.2 lakh per year. Balance is carried forward.

5. **WRONG:** "Section 80EEA is a universal deduction for home loan interest."
   **CORRECT:** It has strict eligibility conditions (loan date, stamp duty limit, first-time buyer).
