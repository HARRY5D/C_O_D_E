# Old Regime vs New Regime — Complete Comparison
# Source: Finance Act 2023, Finance Act 2024 | FY 2025-26 | AY 2026-27

## Overview

India has two parallel income tax regimes. The **New Regime** is now the **default** since FY 2023-24. Taxpayers must **opt-in** to the Old Regime.

---

## Tax Slabs Comparison

### Old Regime

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

### New Regime (FY 2025-26 — Budget 2025)

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

---

## Key Differences

| Feature | Old Regime | New Regime |
|---|---|---|
| Default | No | **Yes (from FY 2023-24)** |
| Standard Deduction | ₹50,000 | **₹75,000** |
| Section 80C (₹1.5L) | ✅ | ❌ |
| Section 80D (Health Insurance) | ✅ | ❌ |
| HRA Exemption | ✅ | ❌ |
| NPS 80CCD(1B) ₹50K | ✅ | ❌ |
| NPS 80CCD(2) Employer | ✅ | ✅ |
| Section 24(b) Home Loan Interest | ✅ | ❌ |
| LTA Exemption | ✅ | ❌ |
| Rebate u/s 87A | Up to ₹5L → nil tax | **Up to ₹12L → nil tax** |

---

## Rebate u/s 87A

| Regime | Income Limit | Max Rebate |
|---|---|---|
| Old Regime | ₹5,00,000 | ₹12,500 |
| New Regime | **₹12,00,000** | **₹60,000** |

**Important**: Under New Regime, if income ≤ ₹12 lakh → effectively **zero tax** (excluding special rate income).

---

## When is Old Regime Better?

Old Regime is beneficial when total deductions exceed a break-even threshold:

| Income | Approximate Deduction Needed to Prefer Old |
|---|---|
| ₹8 Lakh | ~₹1,50,000 |
| ₹12 Lakh | ~₹3,00,000 |
| ₹15 Lakh | ~₹3,75,000 |
| ₹20 Lakh | ~₹4,50,000 |

---

## Who Should Choose New Regime?

1. Young earners with fewer investments
2. Those with no home loan or HRA
3. Those with income ≤ ₹12 Lakh (zero tax after rebate)
4. Employees who don't invest heavily under 80C

## Who Should Choose Old Regime?

1. High-deduction investors (₹2L+ in 80C + 80D + NPS)
2. Those with home loans (₹2L interest deduction)
3. Those paying significant rent in metro cities
4. Senior citizens with high medical expenses

---

## Switching Between Regimes

- **Salaried individuals**: Can switch every year
- **Business/Profession income**: Can switch back to Old Regime only **once** (then permanently on New)
- Must declare choice at the time of filing ITR
