# Presumptive Taxation — FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27
# Source: Income Tax Act 1961, Finance Act 2025

Under Indian tax law, presumptive taxation schemes are designed to simplify compliance for small taxpayers, freelancers, professionals, and small businesses by allowing them to declare taxable profit as a flat percentage of their gross receipts/turnover without needing to maintain extensive books of accounts.

---

## Section 44ADA: Presumptive Taxation for Professionals & Freelancers

### Applicability
Applicable to resident Indian individuals and partnership firms (excluding LLPs) engaged in specified professions:
- Legal, Medical, Engineering, Architectural
- Accountancy, Technical Consultancy, Interior Decoration
- Specified IT professionals, freelancers, and independent consultants

### Key Rules
- **Gross Receipts Limit:** Up to **₹75 Lakhs** per financial year (increased from ₹50 Lakhs, provided cash transactions do not exceed 5% of total receipts).
- **Presumptive Taxable Profit:** Flat **50% of gross receipts** is considered as taxable business income.
- **Deemed Expenses:** The remaining 50% is legally deemed to cover all business expenses (rent, utilities, equipment depreciation, telephone, travel, interest, etc.). No separate deduction for business expenses is allowed under sections 30 to 38.

### Regime Comparison for Section 44ADA
- **Old Tax Regime:** 
  - Presumptive taxable profit = 50% of Gross Receipts.
  - Taxpayer **can claim Chapter VI-A deductions** (Section 80C, 80D, NPS, etc.) and home loan interest (Section 24b) from this 50% presumptive income.
  - Standard Deduction (₹50,000) and HRA exemption do **not** apply (these are exclusively for salaried individuals).
- **New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC):**
  - Presumptive taxable profit = 50% of Gross Receipts.
  - **No Chapter VI-A deductions or home loan interest** can be claimed against the business income.
  - Standard Deduction (₹75,000) does **not** apply.

## Section 44AD: Presumptive Taxation for Small Businesses

### Applicability
Applicable to resident individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms (excluding LLPs) engaged in business, except:
- Professionals covered under Section 44ADA
- Commission, brokerage, or agency businesses
- Carriage of goods (covered under Section 44AE)

### Key Rules
- **Gross Turnover Limit:** Up to **₹3 Crores** per financial year (increased from ₹2 Crores, provided cash receipts do not exceed 5% of total turnover).
- **Presumptive Taxable Profit Rates:**
  - **6% of turnover** for receipts received via digital modes (UPI, net banking, credit/debit card, A/c payee cheque, etc.).
  - **8% of turnover** for receipts received in cash.
- **Regime Comparison:** Same as 44ADA (Chapter VI-A deductions allowed under Old Regime, blocked under New Regime).

## Example Case Study (Gross Receipts: ₹40 Lakhs Freelancer)

| Item | Old Regime (Sec 44ADA) | New Regime (Sec 44ADA) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross Receipts | ₹40,00,000 | ₹40,00,000 |
| Presumptive Profit (50%) | ₹20,00,000 | ₹20,00,000 |
| Standard Deduction | ₹0 | ₹0 |
| Chapter VI-A Deductions | Allowed (e.g. ₹1.5L 80C + 50k NPS) | Blocked (₹0) |
| Net Taxable Income (assuming ₹2L deductions) | ₹18,00,000 | ₹20,00,000 |
| **Tax Calculated (including 4% Cess)** | **₹4,29,000** (assuming ₹0 deductions) | **₹2,08,000** (flat) |

*Conclusion:* The New Tax Regime results in ₹2,08,000 tax, which saves the freelancer ₹2,21,000 compared to the Old Regime tax of ₹4,29,000 (when no deductions are claimed).
